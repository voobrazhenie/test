## Design Specificity Verdict

**The composition is authored for this product. The skin system is only two-thirds authored.**

**LLM assessment (unanchored).** The layout could not be lifted onto an unrelated product: the quality row → extension row → mode row → keybed → strum → history stack is a harmonic signal chain rendered as vertical geography, and the 7-key keybed with computer key-caps printed under the note names is a decision only a chord synth would make. That is not a card grid in a music costume.

Per skin: **Classic** is authored — the cream/coral/amber-VFD read is coherent, and the 4px hard bottom edge collapsing under a 4px `translateY` at 40ms is real contact simulation, not decoration. **Field Unit** is the most authored thing in the file: walnut end cheeks, four screws with *different slot angles*, rust bloom, phosphor CRT, engraved hairline headings, a cat sticker. **Neo-Brutal** is authored — 3px strokes, hard offset shadows, flattened gradients, and three per-row fills that finally make the three rows read as three different kinds of thing. **Lo-Fi 8-Bit** is half-authored (mono face, collapsed radii, scanlined GameBoy LCD — but the composition is untouched). **Vintage Analog is a hue rotation of Classic**: identical geometry, type, radii and rhythm, plus a grain overlay and four ~10%-opacity screws invisible at viewing distance. Field Unit and Neo-Brutal prove the token layer can carry structural change, which is exactly what makes Vintage look like a placeholder that shipped.

## Overall Impression

The instrument itself is good. The press physics, the five-channel chord confirmation, and the token architecture behind Field Unit and Neo-Brutal are better craft than most web audio toys ever get. And then the whole thing is reachable only by mouse and by a set of letter shortcuts documented nowhere a first-timer will look.

The single biggest opportunity is not visual. **13 of 16 state-bearing controls are dead to Enter and Space, 12 piano keys are unfocusable `<div>`s, and the file contains zero focus styles.** You have built a keyboard-playable instrument that a keyboard user cannot operate. Fixing that costs less than any of the skins did and moves three heuristics at once.

## What's Working

**1. The press physics are real, not decorative.** `.obtn` carries `box-shadow: 0 4px 0 var(--coral-dark)` — a hard bottom edge, not a blur — and `:active` applies `translateY(4px)` while collapsing that shadow to zero. The button travels exactly the distance its own edge occupied, in 40ms, below the threshold where you read it as animation rather than contact.

**2. One gesture, five synchronized confirmations.** `chordOn()` drives the chord name, the note list, the mini-keyboard pitch classes, the strum resegmentation and the history push from a single event, with nothing lagging or contradicting. For an instrument, where the entire promise is responsiveness, this is the most important thing to get right and it is right.

**3. The token layer has genuine range.** Per-row accent overrides (`#qualityRow { --coral: … }`) recolor every button state — rest, held, latched, LED, shadow — from three declarations. That is why Field Unit can go brick/burnt-orange/olive and still look like one designed object rather than a find-and-replace.

## Priority Issues

### [P0] The instrument cannot be operated by keyboard, and focus points at controls that do not respond

**Why it matters.** Both assessments landed on this independently. Measured: `Enter` and `Space` do nothing on all six steppers, all four qualities, all four extensions, all four engines, and all four mode toggles — every state-bearing control binds `pointerdown` only, never `click`. Six of those (`#voiceDown/Up`, `#octDown/Up`, `#bpmDown/Up`) are in the tab order and completely dead, which is worse than not exposing them. The 12 piano keys, 12 mini-keys, strum pads and replayable history cells are `<div>`s with no role, tabindex or name — absent from the accessibility tree entirely. The file has **zero** author `:focus`/`:focus-visible` rules, so everything relies on the UA ring, which is invisible in Neo-Brutal (it merges into the 3px black border) and weak on Field Unit's olive. Overlays have no `role="dialog"`, no `aria-modal`, no focus move on open, and the first three Tabs after opening land on buttons *behind* the overlay. This is WCAG 2.1.1, 2.4.7 and 4.1.2 simultaneously.

**Fix.** Add `click` handlers alongside every `pointerdown` (guard with `e.detail === 0` to catch keyboard-synthesized clicks without double-firing). Convert `.key` divs to `<button aria-label="C, white key">`. Add one explicit focus token — `:focus-visible { outline: 3px solid var(--focus-ring); outline-offset: 3px }` — with `--focus-ring` defined per skin. Add `aria-pressed` to the four mode toggles and `role="radiogroup"`/`aria-checked` to the engine quartet. On overlay open, move focus to the close button, trap it, and restore on close.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable audit`

### [P0] Selection state across eight buttons rides on a 6px dot — and "on" looks darker than "off"

**Why it matters.** `.obtn.active` applies `translateY(4px)` plus a *darker* gradient, so the engaged buttons read as recessed and dead while the idle ones read as bright and live. The engine quartet is worse: selected vs unselected differ by a 3% luminance delta plus a 6px dot. In Field Unit all eight go identical olive; in Neo-Brutal the selected engine is the only black button among four white ones, which reads as *disabled*. ARP silently changes how every subsequent chord sounds; BASS silently changes what MIDI you export. A user who misreads which four of eight are on will blame the instrument. Compounding it, one label — "Sound & Mode" — covers a 4-way radio group *and* four independent latches with no gap, rule or colour break between them.

**Fix.** Split the row into two labelled groups, "Sound" (radio) and "Mode" (toggles). Carry selection on *fill* — selected gets a full accent fill with a high-contrast label, unselected gets the outline treatment — and keep the LED as secondary confirmation only. Verify per skin so Field Unit and Neo-Brutal don't collapse the distinction.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable layout`

### [P1] The primary gesture has 26px touch targets on mobile

**Why it matters.** Measured at 390×844: every `.obtn` is 84×26; all six steppers, both icon buttons and `#playBtn` are 26×26 at *every* viewport and skin; the history inversion arrows are 17×18. iOS minimum is 44×44. This is not a peripheral control — the core interaction is holding a 26px-tall button with one thumb while tapping a key with another. Miss the hold and you get a bare single note with no explanation.

**Fix.** Raise `.obtn` to a 44px minimum on touch and buy the vertical budget by deferring something — the extensions and modes need not both be permanently visible on a phone. Give the steppers and icon buttons a transparent 44px hit area via a `::before` overlay without changing their visual size.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable adapt`

### [P2] Help documents about 30% of the product, and the one inline hint has never rendered

**Why it matters.** `#dispNotes` ships with `"hold a chord quality · tap a key"` — the only inline onboarding copy in the product — and `refreshIdleDisplay()` overwrites it synchronously on init. Sampled at 0/50/200/600ms it is always `["C","C3"]`. **No human has ever seen that hint.** Meanwhile the help overlay is one prose blob with no heading or grouping, covering the chord gesture and the key map and nothing else; the least discoverable controls in the product are precisely the ones it ignores. Assessment B also found **0 headings and 0 landmark regions** in the accessibility tree, and nine controls named by a bare glyph — three separate controls all announce as `"−"`.

**Fix.** Render the idle hint when `lastChord === null` instead of unconditionally overwriting. Restructure help into three headed sections — Play a chord / Shape the sound / Capture — with a `<kbd>` shortcut table, and hide the mouse-and-keyboard section under 640px. Give the glyph buttons real `aria-label`s.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable clarify`

## Persona Red Flags

**Alex (power user).** Voicing cycles **7** states through a `−/+` stepper with no list, no preview and no shortcut — comparing DROP 2 against WIDE is five clicks each way, by ear, with no A/B. Mouse-clicking a quality **latches it silently**; Assessment A latched `M7` once and got `Dmmaj7 / Esusmaj7 / Fmaj7 / Gdimmaj7` for four consecutive chords before noticing. Chord spelling reads `Dmmaj7` where a working musician expects `Dm(maj7)`, and `dim6`/`sus6` aren't standard at all. MIDI export gives **zero confirmation** — the generated filename `chords-C-Dm-Esus4-F.mid` is a lovely touch he will never see. No undo, no per-cell delete, no reorder, and history holds only 4 slots with no scroll-back.

**Jordan (first-timer).** Lands on 43 simultaneous controls with no primary action and **no onboarding text at all**, because the one hint string is overwritten before paint. The empty history is a ~70px black slab holding four 1.78:1 dots — it reads as a broken display on an instrument that hasn't made a sound yet. The strum plate says "drag across the **pads**" over a single undivided bar. ▶ on an empty sequence looks armed and does nothing, with no message. She will press ARP, hear something change, and find nothing in help explaining what — and the button will now look *darker*, i.e. less active. On mobile the two-finger hold-and-tap gesture is never explained; the help talks about a mouse.

**Sam (accessibility-dependent).** **Cannot play a chord with Tab and Enter** — verified across 13 of 16 controls. The 12 piano keys are `<div>`s invisible to screen readers. **Zero focus indicators in the file**; the UA ring is invisible in Neo-Brutal. Six focusable-but-dead steppers. No `aria-pressed` on any toggle and no `aria-checked` on the engine radio, so there is no way to know ARP is on — the LED is a CSS `::after`, invisible to AT. Overlays are not dialogs: focus never enters them, is not trapped, and is not returned on close. Contrast fails per the table above. No `prefers-reduced-motion` guard on the MIDI flash, the strum `.lit` transform or the history glow.

## Minor Observations

- The `.hinv` ▲▼ buttons sit in the gutter *between* history cells, so the pair belonging to one chord reads as belonging to its neighbour. Proximity contradicts ownership.
- `.controls` wraps badly at 390px in every skin: ⚙ lands far-left of a new row and ? far-right, ~230px of dead space between. It reads as a layout accident because it is one.
- At **320×568 the device overflows vertically** (690–781px against a 568 viewport) — outside the tested range, but small phones exist.
- `#midiBtn`'s `title` is 230 characters of hedged prose. That's a spec note, not UI copy.
- The version pill carries more visual weight than any section heading in the product.
- `#playBtn` never swaps ▶ for ■ while playing; it only gains a glow.
- The mini-keyboard inside the display is an amber blob at rest — its function is unreadable until a chord plays, and in Lo-Fi and Neo-Brutal the unlit keys nearly match the display background.
- `#installBtn` is hidden until `beforeinstallprompt` fires, leaving "Works offline once installed…" dangling with no adjacent control on every non-Chromium browser.

## Questions to Consider

1. **Why is Vintage Analog a skin?** It shares 100% of Classic's geometry, type and rhythm. Field Unit and Neo-Brutal prove the token layer carries structural change. Either give Vintage a real thesis — rotary knobs, a different keybed, a nameplate — or cut it and ship four skins that each earn their card.
2. **What is the single primary action, and can you point at it on screen?** "Hold a quality and tap a key" is a two-handed compound gesture whose halves sit 300px apart with three control rows between them. What would this look like if the quality buttons lived *on* the keybed?
3. **You built one of the most careful press simulations I've seen on the web — and then made "on" look darker than "off."** Was the LED meant to carry latch state, or did it inherit momentary-press semantics and quietly get overloaded? Those are two different signals sharing one 6px dot.
4. **Who is the MIDI OUT section for, and why does it outrank the strum plate in reading order?** It is the most technical thing on the surface, needs third-party software, is hidden on mobile, and sits directly in the path of every first-timer who will never use it.
5. **The empty history bar is ~70px of near-black nothing under an instrument at rest.** If the first impression is "is this broken?", should it collapse into a line of prompt copy that expands once a chord exists?
6. **You shipped a computer-keyboard layout good enough to actually play with, and printed the key caps on the hardware.** Why does it live only inside a modal, and why does the product never tell a keyboard user it exists?
