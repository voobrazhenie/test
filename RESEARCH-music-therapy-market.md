# Playful ambient music-making as a wellness product — market research

**Date:** August 2026
**Question asked:** what are the most successful business models and products globally, and which are candidates to copy?

---

## 1. The single most important finding

**"Music + wellness" and "playful music-making" are two different economies, and almost nobody has successfully joined them.**

| | Passive listening wellness | Active playful creation |
|---|---|---|
| Examples | Endel, Brain.fm, Calm, Spiritune, LUCID | Incredibox, Bloom, cozy games, Ableton Note |
| What the user does | Presses play, receives | Makes something |
| Business model | Subscription ($50–70/yr) | One-time purchase ($4–10) or premium game |
| Growth engine | Paid acquisition, ASO, partnerships | Organic virality, UGC, TikTok/YouTube |
| Health claims | Central to positioning | Absent |
| Category health | **Maturing, subscribers declining** | **Growing fast** |
| Money raised | $20M+ rounds available | Mostly bootstrapped |

Your idea — playful, ambient, no theory required, therapeutic, aesthetic — sits **in the active column** but is being *positioned* with the language of the passive column.

That gap is either the opportunity or the graveyard. The research says it is currently a graveyard, and the reason is specific and fixable. Read section 6 before section 5.

---

## 2. Scoreboard: who is actually making money

### Passive listening / functional audio

| Company | Model | Scale | Read |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Endel** | Sub, ~$6.99/mo, ~$49.99/yr, ~$90 lifetime | $22.1M raised (True Ventures, Waverley); 4M+ downloads | Best-in-class execution of the AI-soundscape thesis. Heavy label/brand partnership strategy. Still not a household name after 8 years. |
| **Brain.fm** | Sub, freemium tiers | **~$7.1M revenue (2026)**, ~2M downloads, ~100k MAU | Founded 2014. Twelve years to $7M. This is the realistic ceiling for a well-run science-branded audio sub without consumer-brand spend. |
| **Calm** | Sub | ~3.5M subscribers in 2025 — **down 500k YoY** | Category leader shrinking. Raised prices and split out a separate Sleep sub in 2025/26 — classic late-stage monetization squeeze. |
| **Headspace** | Sub | ~$140M (2025), 2M subs — **down 300k YoY** | Same signal. |
| **Spiritune** | Sub + B2B | **$1.7M raised total** | Closest thing to a literal "music therapy app". Real peer-reviewed study (PLoS One), music-therapist-designed. Tiny. The credential-and-evidence route is *slow*. |
| **LUCID** | Digital therapeutic / clinical | $3M raised; LUC-101 for Alzheimer's agitation; RCT with Toronto Metropolitan | The regulated route. Multi-year, capital-hungry, but defensible. Not a solo-founder path. |

**Takeaway:** the wellness-audio subscription category is *mature and contracting at the top*. Calm and Headspace both lost subscribers. Brain.fm took 12 years to reach $7M. Spiritune raised $1.7M in total. This is not a category where a new entrant wins by being slightly better.

### Active creation / playful

| Product | Model | Scale | Read |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Incredibox** (So Far So Good) | **One-time purchase (~$4–5)** + free web demo | **3.3M paid downloads; 106M+ free web players.** ~$70k/mo (2022 estimate, almost certainly much higher post-2024) | **The single best template for your idea.** See section 5. |
| **Sprunki** | Fan mods, free | Viral phenomenon 2024–25 that massively re-accelerated Incredibox | Proof that a UGC/remix layer is the growth engine, not the marketing budget. |
| **Bloom** (Eno / Chilvers, Opal) | One-time purchase | Cult classic since 2008, "Bloom: 10 Worlds" edition | Closest aesthetic and philosophical ancestor to your idea. Tap-anywhere generative ambient, no theory. Artistically canonical, commercially modest — it never built a growth engine. |
| **Endlesss** | **Sub $12/mo** | **SHUT DOWN May 2024** | The cautionary tale. Collaborative music-making, beloved, Kickstarter-funded, led by a respected artist (Tim Exile). Subscription on a creative toy killed it. Now revived non-commercially under Imogen Heap's HabLab. |
| **Loóna** | Sub | VC-backed (Hoxton, Elefund, Elysium, Natalia Vodianova) | **The most interesting hybrid.** Interactive *activity* (coloring in, low-skill, tactile) + sleep framing + strong art direction. Apple has featured its design. Proof that "gentle interactive activity → wellness outcome" can raise money. |
| **Wotja** | Freemium, Lite free forever | Niche, long-lived | Generative ambient for enthusiasts. Shows the enthusiast ceiling is low. |
| **Artiphon Orba** | Hardware, $149.99 | **$1.4M Kickstarter** | "Instrument for everyone" demand is real and people pay $150 for a *physical* version of your premise. |

### The elephant

**Suno**: ~$300M ARR, 2M paid subscribers, $2.45B valuation, $250M Series C. Text-to-music generation. Not your competitor, but it resets user expectations about what "making music without skill" means, and it hoovers up the investor attention in this space.

## 3. Market size numbers (treat with suspicion)

- Music therapy market: ~$2.84B (2024) → ~$6.21B (2033), ~9% CAGR
- Sound therapy market: ~$2.70B (2026) → ~$4.60B (2033), 8% CAGR
- Cozy games: ~$1.1B (2025) → ~$1.65B (2032), 6% CAGR
- Wellness app ARPU: ~$21/user

⚠️ **Caveat:** the music-therapy and sound-therapy figures come from SEO-driven market-research vendors (Verified Market Reports, Coherent, SkyQuest, Precision). They are not reliable and mostly measure *in-person clinical music therapy services*, not apps. Do not put these in a pitch deck as-is. The cozy-games and RevenueCat numbers are more trustworthy.

**The genuinely useful signal:** on Steam, use of "cozy" as a primary descriptor among games earning >$100k lifetime **grew 675% between 2022 and 2025** (0.4% → 3.1% of high-earning titles), with ~375 cozy-tagged games launched in 2025. Comfort-first, aesthetic-first, low-stakes interactive experiences are where the cultural and commercial momentum actually is.

## 4. Monetization benchmarks you should design against

From RevenueCat's State of Subscription Apps 2026:

- Subscription apps: **13.8% D30 retention** vs 5.3% ad-monetized; 7.9% vs 1.7% at D90
- Median trial→paid: **25.6%**; Health & Fitness leads at **~35%**
- **Hard paywalls convert ~5x better than freemium** (10.7% vs 2.1% median D35)
- Long trials (17–32 days) convert at **~42.5%** vs 25.5% for <4-day trials
- Cheap annual plans retain **~36% after a year**; expensive monthly plans retain **6.7%**
- Median prices: ~$7.48/week, ~$12.99/month, ~$38.42/year
- Subscription ARPU is ~4.6x ad-only

**Design implications:**
1. If you go subscription, go **annual and cheap** ($30–40/yr), not monthly. Monthly on a "toy" churns catastrophically — this is what killed Endlesss.
2. A **long trial** (2–4 weeks) converts nearly twice as well as a short one, and is philosophically consistent with a calm product.
3. Health & Fitness framing genuinely converts better (~35% vs 25.6%) — this is the *real* commercial argument for the wellness positioning, not the therapeutic claim itself.

## 5. The Incredibox playbook — the closest thing to "copy-paste"

You said you'd consider copying a working model. This is the one to copy.

**What they did:**
1. **Free web version as the entire marketing budget.** 106M+ players on the web demo. No paid acquisition. The free version *is* the funnel and the brand.
2. **Cheap one-time paid app** (~$4–5) as the conversion. 3.3M paid downloads. No subscription, no churn, no retention treadmill.
3. **Distinct "worlds"/versions** as the content cadence — each new version is a new art direction + new sound set. This is *exactly* the skin/world architecture you already built in Inspiration Machine.
4. **Share-a-mix as the viral loop.** Every creation is a shareable artifact. Users market the product by using it.
5. **Tiny team, Lyon, France, funded by an unrelated design agency.** Sustainable, not venture-scale — but profitable and enduring since 2009.
6. **They let the mod scene (Sprunki) explode** rather than suppressing it, and it re-ignited the franchise in 2024–25.

**Why it maps onto your idea almost perfectly:**
- No music theory required ✅
- Playful ✅
- Extremely strong art direction as the differentiator ✅
- Output is inherently shareable ✅
- "Worlds" cadence matches your existing six-skin architecture ✅

**What you'd change:** ambient instead of beatbox, calm instead of energetic, and a wellness/regulation-of-state framing instead of a pure toy framing.

**Combine it with the Loóna insight:** Loóna proved you can take a low-skill tactile activity (coloring), wrap it in exceptional art direction, attach it to a wellness outcome (sleep), and raise venture money. Loóna is Incredibox's mechanic logic applied to the wellness market. **Your idea is Incredibox × Loóna.** That intersection is real and, as far as this research found, unoccupied for *ambient music-making specifically*.

## 6. The thing that kills products in this space

**Endlesss died charging $12/month for a creative toy.**

The pattern is consistent across the evidence:

- **Creative toys monetize as purchases.** Incredibox: one-time, thriving since 2009. Bloom: one-time, alive since 2008. Ableton Live: perpetual license. Koala: one-time.
- **Wellness monetizes as subscription** — but only when the user has a *recurring problem* (I can't sleep, I can't focus) that recurs nightly and makes them re-open the app.

A creative toy has no recurring problem. The user makes a beautiful thing, feels good, and doesn't need you tomorrow. **That is why "playful music-making as a subscription" has no winners.**

**So the central strategic question for your product is not aesthetic and not technical. It is:**

> What makes someone open this again tomorrow night?

Loóna answered it with "you can't sleep, and this is your bedtime ritual." Endel answered it with "you're working right now." Endlesss never answered it and died.

If you can answer it — a nightly wind-down ritual, a mood check-in that produces a piece of music, a daily "one minute of ambient", a streak of sonic artifacts you accumulate — you get subscription economics on a creative toy, and nobody else has that. If you can't answer it, **price it as a one-time purchase and follow Incredibox exactly.** That is still a very good business and a far lower-risk one.

## 7. Language and regulatory constraints (important, cheap to get wrong)

1. **Do not call it "music therapy."** *Music Therapist – Board Certified (MT-BC)* is a protected credential administered by the CBMT (bachelor's degree + supervised internship + board exam). Marketing a consumer app as "music therapy" without credentialed involvement invites both professional-body friction and FTC-style substantiation problems.

2. **Stay on the "general wellness" side of the FDA line.** An app becomes a regulated medical device when it is *intended to diagnose, treat, cure, mitigate, or prevent* a disease. Relaxation, mindfulness, mood, and general wellbeing claims are outside FDA device regulation. The moment you say "treats anxiety," you are in Class II digital-therapeutic territory — that is LUCID's road: RCTs, years, and millions.

3. **Safe framing:** "calm", "unwind", "wind down", "a moment of quiet", "made by you". Unsafe framing: "therapy", "treats", "clinically proven", "reduces anxiety by X%".

4. **The neuroaesthetics angle is a legitimate asset if used as *design rationale*, not as a health claim.** The credible institutional anchor is Susan Magsamen's International Arts + Mind Lab at Johns Hopkins and the "neuroarts" field. Evidence supports that aesthetic experience shifts stress physiology (amygdala activation down, parasympathetic engagement up). Your friends' expertise is real differentiation — for *how you build it* and for *credibility in a deck* — but it should not become label copy.

5. **Genuine science tailwind for your specific mechanic:** the literature distinguishes active from passive engagement, and finds **active music-making benefits both cognitive *and* psychosocial functioning, while passive listening's benefits are largely restricted to the cognitive domain** — with active participation additionally engaging motor and proprioceptive systems and affecting dopamine/serotonin. In other words: *the entire wellness-audio category is built on the weaker half of the evidence.* That is a genuinely strong, honest, defensible pitch.
   - One caveat the literature also flags: active music-making can introduce performance pressure and perfectionism. **Your "no music theory needed" design is precisely the mitigation for the one known downside.** That is a real, evidence-grounded product principle.

## 8. What you already own

`Inspiration Machine` in this repo is not the same product, but it is a substantial head start:

**Transfers directly:**
- Chord engine — 32 named chords, gesture model of "hold a quality, tap a key". The core "play without theory" problem is *already solved* in code.
- Six-skin architecture over one custom-property contract → this is exactly Incredibox's "worlds" content cadence, already built.
- Voicings, ARP/HOLD/BASS modes, strum plate — the raw material for generative ambient behaviour.
- Single self-contained HTML, no build step, offline PWA → free web version as the marketing engine costs you essentially nothing to ship.

**Does not transfer:**
- Positioning. Inspiration Machine is aimed at songwriters capturing progressions into a DAW. That user has a job to do; your therapy user does not.
- MIDI export / VST3 / DAW integration — irrelevant, arguably harmful, to the wellness user.
- "Findings over generation" principle — you may want the *opposite* here. Therapeutic play wants the instrument to be generous and to make the user sound good; a songwriting tool must not compose for them.
- Visual language. Six skins exist, but the documented critique found contrast failing WCAG AA in four of five skins and 26px touch targets. An aesthetics-led wellness product cannot ship that.

**Honest assessment:** maybe 50–60% of an MVP's engine, close to 0% of its positioning. The chord engine plus the skin architecture is worth real months. Treat it as a component library, not a product to re-skin.

## 9. Recommendation

**Copy Incredibox's business model, Loóna's positioning, and Bloom's aesthetic lineage.**

Concretely:

1. **Free web version, permanently, as the whole funnel.** You already have the single-file PWA architecture to do this for near-zero marginal cost. This is non-negotiable — it is how every winner in the active-creation column grew.
2. **Do not launch on subscription unless you have answered the "why tomorrow" question.** Default to a one-time purchase at $5–8 for the app. If you find the ritual, move to a cheap annual (~$35/yr) with a long (3–4 week) trial.
3. **Make every session produce a shareable artifact** — a loop, a short video with the visuals, a named piece. The visual/aesthetic quality is the share trigger, and it's your stated strength.
4. **"Worlds" as the content cadence,** reusing the skin architecture — each world a complete art + sound direction. This is your retention *and* your PR calendar.
5. **Position on state, not on treatment.** "Make something beautiful for five minutes before bed." Not "music therapy."
6. **Use the neuroaesthetics expertise as a design method and a credibility asset in fundraising** — and, if you want defensibility later, as the basis for one real study (Spiritune's PLoS One paper is the model, and it cost them far less than a regulated trial).

**The strategic bet, stated plainly:** the wellness-audio market is mature, contracting at the top, and built entirely on passive listening — which the evidence says is the weaker intervention. The active-creation market is growing, viral, and cheap to enter, but has no wellness monetization. You are betting you can be the first to bridge them. That bet lives or dies on the ritual question in section 6, not on the art or the tech.

---

## 10. What to validate next (in order)

1. **The ritual question.** Interview 15–20 people about what they actually do in the 30 minutes before bed. Does "make a little music" survive contact with reality, or does it lose to scrolling? This determines your entire business model.
2. **Incredibox's actual current numbers.** The $70k/mo figure is from 2022 and pre-dates the Sprunki explosion. Sensor Tower / Appfigures paid data would give you the real ceiling of the one-time-purchase model. Worth the $100.
3. **Whether "ambient" is too narrow.** Incredibox works partly because beatbox is instantly legible and fun to *watch*. Ambient is beautiful but less immediately shareable. Test the share loop early — it's the growth engine, and ambient is its biggest risk.
4. **Talk to Loóna's trajectory.** They are the closest strategic analogue; their public funding history and current state tells you whether the hybrid raises money.
